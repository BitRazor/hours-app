## Tournament journey so far

### England (Group L winners — unbeaten)

| Stage | Result |
|---|---|
| Group | England 4–2 Croatia |
| Group | England 0–0 Ghana |
| Group | England 2–0 Panama |
| Round of 32 | England 2–1 DR Congo (Kane double, late scare) |
| Round of 16 | England 3–2 Mexico at the Azteca (won with 10 men, Bellingham brace) |

- 11 goals scored, 5 conceded; have scored in three straight but conceded in each of the last two.
- This is England's **11th World Cup quarter-final** — only Brazil and Germany have more. Sobering note: England won just 3 of the previous 10, and conceded multiple goals in 7 of them.

### Norway (Group I runners-up — first ever WC quarter-final)

| Stage | Result |
|---|---|
| Group | Norway 4–1 Iraq |
| Group | Norway 3–2 Senegal |
| Group | Norway 1–4 France (heavily rotated side) |
| Round of 16 | Norway 2–1 Brazil (Haaland brace in the final 11 minutes; Nyland saved a Bruno Guimarães penalty) |

- 12 goals scored, 9 conceded. Norway have **both scored and conceded in all five matches** — chaos merchants.
- Knocking out Brazil (their earliest exit since 1990) is the shock of the tournament.
- Historical caveat: Norway have **never beaten European opposition in a World Cup knockout match**.

  Predicted (4-3-3): Nyland; Ryerson, Ajer, Heggem, Wolfe; Berg, Berge, Ødegaard; Sørloth, Haaland, Nusa.
- **England:** real problems in defence. Quansah **suspended**, Reece James not training (hamstring), Guehi a doubt (hamstring), Henderson out of the tournament (wrist). Djed Spence likely deputises at right-back.
  Predicted (4-2-3-1): Pickford; Spence, Konsa, Guehi (if fit), O'Reilly; Anderson, Rice; Saka, Bellingham, Gordon; Kane.

## Head-to-head

England lead the all-time series 9–2 (1 draw) across 12 meetings, and Norway have failed to score in the last four. But none of those matches featured this Norway generation at full power.

## Brainstorm: how each side wins

**England's path:** control midfield through Rice + Bellingham, use Saka/Gordon against Norway's full-backs (Senegal and Brazil both scored past this back line — everyone scores against Norway), and let Kane's knockout pedigree do the rest. If England score first, Norway must open up, and that's when England's counter (Gordon's pace) becomes lethal.

**Norway's path:** it's all about the Haaland matchup against a patched-up England right/centre defence. Spence–Konsa is an improvised pairing on that side; Guehi is half-fit at best. Haaland has punished far better-organised defences this tournament, and England's quarter-final history (2+ goals conceded in 7 of 10) suggests fragility at exactly this stage. Ødegaard finding Haaland in transition off an English set piece is the nightmare scenario. Miami heat also favours the side that has to run less — that's the counter-punching team.

**The X-factors:**
- England have needed late drama in both knockout games (DR Congo, 10-men win vs Mexico) — resilience, or luck running out?
- Norway "lost" to France while resting players, so they're fresher than the record shows.
- Neutral crowd will be heavily pro-Haaland (he's become the tournament's adopted star since the USMNT exit).

## Verdict

Adjusting Opta slightly for England's defensive injuries (which the pre-match sims may underweight):

> **England 2–1 Norway** — but closer than the ranking gap implies.
> Kane and Bellingham involved in the goals; Haaland scores his 8th of the tournament in a losing effort.
> Confidence: ~55–60% England progress, ~40–45% Norway.
> Upset scoreline if it goes wrong: Norway 2–1, both goals Haaland, one from a set piece.

If it reaches extra time, flip the lean toward Norway — fresher legs, Miami humidity, and the momentum of a nation that has never been here before.
